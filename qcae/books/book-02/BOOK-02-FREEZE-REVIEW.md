## Book II Core Transformation

Book II converts:

```text
"Find me something that does X"
```

into:

```text
Here are the strongest non-duplicative acquisition paths for capability X,
what source actually implements,
what each path depends on,
what can be extracted/reimplemented,
what assumptions and burdens remain,
and exactly what must be proven next.
```

## Book II Frozen Laws

1. Discovery begins from capability semantics.
2. Internal baseline precedes unnecessary external acquisition.
3. Search uses multiple hypotheses, sources, and vocabulary families.
4. Discovery provenance is durable.
5. Candidate identity is canonicalized across discovery sources.
6. Diversity and information gain matter more than raw result count.
7. Search has budgets, saturation measures, and stop rules.
8. Repository intelligence is immutable-revision scoped.
9. Source is authoritative over DeepWiki/LLM explanation.
10. DeepWiki is a comprehension provider, never proof or a constitutional dependency.
11. Structural mapping includes state, build, runtime, interfaces, tests, and dynamic uncertainty.
12. Capability localization includes the full source/dependency/side-effect envelope.
13. Atom-level dependency burden is separated from framework-level burden.
14. Source-level claim support is distinct from runtime/contract/domain proof.
15. The Minimum Extractable Unit is semantically coherent rather than artificially tiny.
16. Specification and interface recovery seek implementation independence.
17. Hidden assumptions become explicit proof targets.
18. Complexity includes long-term ownership and exit costs.
19. The first discovered repository never owns the solution space.
20. Book II terminates with a bounded proving portfolio, not an acquisition approval.

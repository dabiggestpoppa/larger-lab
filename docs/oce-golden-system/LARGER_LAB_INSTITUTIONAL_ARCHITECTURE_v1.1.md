## 0. North Star

Larger Lab is a durable substrate for creating intelligent institutions that can sense, reason, experiment, act, learn, replace their own components, and improve the process by which they learn without surrendering epistemic integrity or operator sovereignty.

OCE is the constitutional and epistemic operating system of that substrate.

The intended moat is not any current model, agent, strategy, dataset, framework, or application. The moat is a self-renewing institutional architecture that:

1. preserves evidence and authority through technological replacement;
2. makes useful knowledge progressively executable;
3. creates healthy reciprocal constraints among differentiated cognitive roles;
4. learns from failures without turning failure history into dogma;
5. actively searches for both answers to known questions and anomalies that reveal unknown questions;
6. can alter its tools, practices, models, ontologies, and portions of its own architecture while preserving lineage, bounded authority, and a stable constitutional identity.

The target is not autonomous self-modification.

> **The target is governed recursive self-reconstruction.**

## 1. The deepest institutional distinction: identity versus implementation

The system must remain itself while nearly every implementation beneath it can change.

### 1.1 Replaceable layer

Expected to change frequently:

- models;
- agent runtimes;
- worker frameworks;
- search providers;
- storage engines;
- vector/graph technology;
- backtest engines;
- execution APIs;
- data vendors;
- strategy families;
- feature libraries;
- user interfaces.

### 1.2 Slowly mutable layer

May change after evidence and review:

- capability contracts;
- research methods;
- routing policy;
- knowledge schemas;
- domain ontologies;
- search/refinery policy;
- learning-promotion criteria;
- institutional health metrics.

### 1.3 Constitutional identity layer

Changes only through explicit operator-governed amendment:

- operator sovereignty;
- explicit authority boundaries;
- evidence before consequential belief/action;
- provenance preservation;
- recoverable history;
- independent validation for consequential claims;
- ability for external reality to contradict canon;
- separation of research from capital authority;
- runtime/model replaceability;
- inability of an agent to expand its own authority by learning or self-modification.

**Rule:** constitutionalize principles and invariants, not product names or temporary techniques.

## 2. Intelligence is a property of the circuit

Larger Lab shall not define institutional intelligence as the capability of one model or one agent.

The relevant object is the closed cybernetic circuit:

```text
OPERATOR
   <-> COMPANION
   <-> EXECUTIVE REASONER
   <-> OCE STATE / EVIDENCE / AUTHORITY
   <-> DIFFERENTIATED WORKERS + SCIENCE AGENTS
   <-> DOMAIN SENSORS + DETERMINISTIC KERNELS
   <-> EXTERNAL WORLD
   <-> OCE
```

A stronger model may improve one node. A stronger institution improves the topology, boundaries, feedback, memory, evidence quality, and error correction of the whole circuit.

### 2.1 Shared reality, differentiated cognition

Agents should share:

- canonical facts;
- evidence;
- definitions;
- authority;
- artifact identities;
- state.

Agents may intentionally differ in:

- hypotheses;
- priors;
- methods;
- role objectives;
- source exposure;
- model family;
- review perspective.

**Global agreement is never accepted as a substitute for external evidence.**

## 3. The institutional attractor problem

Any repeated feedback ecology develops stable behavioral tendencies whether designers explicitly intend them or not.

Examples of unhealthy institutional attractors:

- operator approval over truth;
- backtest profit over scientific validity;
- activity over information gain;
- novelty over consolidation;
- safety theater over honest uncertainty;
- consensus over independent reasoning;
- historical canon over anomalous evidence.

Therefore agent alignment is not a prompt or one training phase. It is partly the geometry of the surrounding institution.

### 3.1 Constitutional alignment

Hard consequence boundaries are externally enforced:

- credentials;
- privacy;
- destructive mutations;
- deployment;
- broker/exchange access;
- capital;
- authority expansion;
- irreversible actions.

### 3.2 Organic institutional alignment

Epistemic and work tendencies are cultivated through repeated feedback:

- truth-seeking;
- evidence preference;
- curiosity;
- uncertainty disclosure;
- falsification;
- reproducibility;
- restraint;
- efficient use of resources;
- willingness to stop;
- learning from failure.

The constitution forms the outer wall. The operating ecology cultivates what grows inside it.

## 4. Reciprocal constraint instead of one global optimizer

The institution must not broadcast the same objective to every subsystem.

Durable profit is a whole-system economic objective. Individual components should optimize functions that reciprocally constrain one another.

```text
DISCOVERY      <-> VALIDATION
novelty            evidence

RESEARCH       <-> EXECUTION REALITY
possibility        realizability

OPPORTUNITY    <-> RISK
return             survival

AUTONOMY       <-> AUTHORITY
adaptation          consequence bounds

COHERENCE      <-> EXTERNAL CONTRADICTION
stability           correction

SPEED          <-> REPRODUCIBILITY
throughput          causal legibility
```

No component is permitted to define the institution's entire objective function.

This creates a higher-order system whose quality arises from reciprocal limitation rather than one metric being maximized until it Goodharts the institution.

## 5. ConstraintField v2 — from admissible actions to evolving state landscape

`ConstraintField` remains the universal decision primitive but is extended.

A field contains:

- current objective;
- current state;
- candidate states;
- admissible transitions;
- authority boundaries;
- dependencies;
- budgets;
- evidence requirements;
- active negative knowledge;
- unresolved anomalies;
- uncertainty;
- known failure surfaces;
- reversibility;
- state age / evidence freshness;
- conditions that preserve the current regime;
- conditions that require reopening or transformation.

### 5.1 Resolution principle

As valid constraints accumulate, the admissible transition set should shrink.

### 5.2 Transformation principle

When repeated high-quality contradictions cannot be resolved within the current field, the system must be capable of escalating from **task repair** to **model/ontology repair** instead of endlessly patching local symptoms.

## 6. Two kinds of recovery

### 6.1 Homeostatic recovery

Use when the underlying model remains valid.

Examples:

- process crash;
- corrupted local service;
- failed worker;
- bad deployment;
- transient provider outage;
- deterministic regression.

Goal: restore a verified known-good state.

### 6.2 Heterostatic recovery

Use when repeated evidence suggests the previous known-good state no longer fits reality.

Examples:

- strategy mechanism decays despite correct implementation;
- repeated worker failure implicates the task/runtime abstraction;
- a market regime invalidates historical assumptions;
- a research method systematically generates leakage;
- a domain ontology repeatedly fails to classify important observations.

Goal: construct and verify a new stable regime.

**Failure must be classified by the deepest layer of structure it plausibly implicates.**

## 7. Epistemic tension and transformation

The institution requires a structured way to distinguish ordinary error from accumulated paradigm pressure.

Define `EpistemicTension` as an operational diagnostic, not a metaphysical quantity.

Possible components:

- unresolved contradiction count weighted by evidence quality;
- prediction/residual error persistence;
- exception-rate growth;
- ontology `UNRESOLVED_PATTERN` accumulation;
- repeated manual overrides;
- dependency on ad-hoc patches;
- disagreement between independent evidence classes;
- inability of current mechanism to explain new observations.

The exact metric is domain-specific and must be validated before automation.

### 7.1 Stabilize / transform modes

The institution has two broad cognitive modes:

**STABILIZE** — protect a well-supported structure while processing ordinary noise.

**TRANSFORM** — open alternatives when evidence indicates the current structure cannot absorb contradiction economically.

This is inspired by the Zeno/Anti-Zeno stabilization/transformation framing in the supplied papers, but OCE does not import their quantum or consciousness claims.

## 8. Governed transformation protocol

No agent may unilaterally rewrite a high-level institutional model.

A transformation protocol uses differentiated roles:

```text
STEWARD
protect strongest existing structure and evidence

CHALLENGER
construct strongest contradiction / failure case

EXPLORER
propose alternative models or ontology

AUDITOR
run discriminating tests independently

INTEGRATOR
identify minimal reconstruction that explains more with acceptable cost

OPERATOR / AUTHORIZED GATE
ratify high-level change where required
```

The same underlying model instance may fill multiple roles only where independence is not required. High-consequence transformations require context/model/source independence proportionate to impact.

### 8.1 Punctuated self-improvement

Self-reconstruction proceeds in epochs:

```text
STABLE OPERATING EPOCH
 -> collect experience and anomalies
 -> TRANSFORMATION WINDOW
 -> sandbox competing reconstructions
 -> independent validation
 -> CONSOLIDATION
 -> new stable epoch
```

Continuous unrestricted self-modification is prohibited because it destroys causal legibility and makes improvement impossible to validate.

## 9. Epistemic viscosity

Different layers change at different speeds.

Suggested ordering from low to high resistance:

1. task hypotheses;
2. search priorities;
3. runtime routing;
4. agent practices/skills;
5. capability implementations;
6. research methods;
7. domain models;
8. ontologies;
9. authority model;
10. constitutional invariants.

Higher-layer changes require stronger evidence, wider AffectedSurface review, longer observation, and more operator authority.

This prevents both architectural ossification and uncontrolled novelty cascades.

## 10. Epistemic lifecycle — knowledge is not binary

Institutional knowledge moves through explicit states.

```text
OBSERVED
 -> CANDIDATE
 -> TESTED
 -> PROMOTED
 -> ACTIVE
 -> REVALIDATED

or

 -> CHALLENGED
 -> DEMOTED
 -> DORMANT
 -> REACTIVATED

or

 -> SUPERSEDED
```

Every transition preserves provenance and reason.

### 10.1 Active / dormant / archival distinction

**ACTIVE KNOWLEDGE** — routinely influences decisions.

**DORMANT KNOWLEDGE** — not currently weighted by default but eligible for reactivation if state/reopen conditions return.

**ARCHIVAL EVIDENCE** — historically immutable/recoverable but not automatically loaded into cognition.

This permits forgetting in active cognition without historical erasure.

## 11. NegativeKnowledge v2 — failure without dogma

Every negative-knowledge record should include:

- exact claim/path rejected;
- evidence;
- scope;
- market/runtime/environment state;
- assumptions;
- confidence;
- causal interpretation if supported;
- what remains unknown;
- `reopen_conditions`;
- superseding evidence if later reactivated.

A negative result without scope or reopen conditions is dangerous because it can become an anti-innovation attractor.

## 12. Epistemic metabolism

Institutional memory requires a lifecycle analogous to metabolism rather than unlimited retention in active cognition.

```text
INGEST
 -> NORMALIZE
 -> CONSOLIDATE
 -> COMPRESS
 -> PROMOTE / DORMANT / REJECT
 -> REVALIDATE
 -> PRUNE FROM ACTIVE CONTEXT
 -> RETAIN PROVENANCE
```

### 12.1 Healthy forgetting

The system may remove an object from default retrieval because:

- a superior abstraction subsumes it;
- evidence is stale;
- a regime ended;
- capability disappeared;
- information value fell;
- repeated validation failed;
- context cost exceeds current usefulness.

The evidence record is not deleted merely to reduce context.

### 12.2 Anti-hyperthymesia rule

A system that remembers everything with equal cognitive weight becomes less intelligent as history grows.

The objective is **recoverable history with selective active influence**.

## 13. Anomaly preservation and ontological openness

The system must support observations that do not fit existing categories.

Never force every novel observation into the closest current ontology.

Introduce an explicit state:

`UNRESOLVED_PATTERN`

This means:

- observation is real enough to preserve;
- existing categories are insufficient;
- no new mechanism is yet authorized;
- related anomalies may be clustered later.

### 13.1 Two discovery modes

**Question-driven discovery:** internal EvidenceGap/CapabilityGap generates SearchDemand.

**Anomaly-driven discovery:** world observation violates expectation before the system knew what question to ask.

A mature institution needs both.

## 14. Structured dissent and epistemic biodiversity

Institutional intelligence requires differentiation.

Some research tasks should deliberately preserve:

- independent models;
- separate context;
- distinct source sets;
- different priors;
- alternate mathematical formalisms;
- adversarial hypotheses.

Consensus is a result to explain, not a truth signal by itself.

### 14.1 Epistemic friction

For high-consequence or high-attractor tasks, immediate synchronization may be harmful.

The system may require:

- fresh-context review;
- independent reconstruction before seeing another agent's conclusion;
- cooling periods for certain ontology changes;
- staged evidence disclosure;
- delayed synthesis.

This intentionally introduces useful cognitive latency to reduce reflective bypass, groupthink, and correlated error.

## 15. Collective intelligence health

The institution should eventually monitor the health of its cognitive ecology, not just task success.

Candidate diagnostics:

- correlated failure rate;
- source concentration;
- model/runtime concentration;
- independent-replication frequency;
- disagreement diversity;
- contradiction detection latency;
- evidence propagation latency;
- anomaly backlog;
- percentage of beliefs with explicit falsifiers/reopen conditions;
- strategy/mechanism-family concentration;
- context cost per resolved EvidenceGap;
- rate of local fixes later revealed as higher-level failures;
- frequency of canonical assumptions challenged;
- proportion of agent conclusions supported by independent evidence.

These are diagnostics, not one optimization score. Maximizing a single institutional-health scalar is prohibited until strong evidence shows that doing so cannot Goodhart the ecology.

## 16. Institutional self-observation

OCE should eventually reconstruct a bounded self-model of the institution itself.

It should be able to state, with evidence:

- what the lab currently believes;
- what remains uncertain;
- which capabilities are weak;
- which roles are over/under-used;
- where research is concentrated;
- where errors are correlated;
- what consumes resources;
- which assumptions have not been challenged recently;
- which knowledge is becoming stale;
- where external reality is diverging from institutional expectation.

This is organizational recursion: the institution observes its own operation and uses that observation to alter future organization.

No self-model is granted epistemic sovereignty. It remains auditable and externally challengeable.

## 17. Cognitive role topology v2

### Operator

Sovereign over constitution, high-impact architecture, capital, and genuinely irreducible ambiguity.

### Hermes / CompanionRuntime

Maintains human-system continuity and translates operator intent without becoming institutional truth.

### PO / ExecutiveReasoner

Allocates attention and resources, resolves ConstraintFields, regulates stabilize/transform mode, and manages institutional topology rather than performing every task.

### Worker Fabric

OpenClaw/Pi/future runtimes perform bounded execution. They are interchangeable implementations behind capability and authority contracts.

### Science Fabric / QCAE

Generates mechanisms, alternatives, falsifiers, and experiment designs but cannot certify its own claims.

### Steward / Challenger / Explorer / Auditor / Integrator

Transformation roles used when institutional beliefs or architecture are being reconstructed. These are roles, not necessarily permanent agents.

```text
RAW SOURCE

 -> OBSERVATION
 -> MECHANISM
 -> EXPERIMENTAL RELATIONSHIP
 -> PRACTICE
 -> PROCEDURE
 -> TEST
```

But every executable promotion remains linked to:

- provenance;
- evidence;
- scope;
- falsifier;
- expiry;
- reopen condition;
- authority class.

Thus the institution gains efficiency without hiding why it behaves as it does.

It must maintain distinction among:

- observations;
- state models;
- mechanisms;
- strategy compositions;
- execution models;

- portfolio effects.

### 19.2 Research Genome without ontology prison

### 19.3 CEREBUS

## 20. External intelligence and anti-absorption doctrine

Larger Lab may learn deeply from an external theory without importing its ontology wholesale.

For every major external framework, the refinery should separate:

1. observations/data;
2. operational methods;
3. falsifiable mechanism claims;
4. architectural metaphors;
5. metaphysical/political interpretation.

Different layers receive different evidence status.

For the supplied Michels works, this architecture adopts as design inspirations:

- attractor-aware systems thinking;
- recursion and self-reference;
- distinction between behavior and internal organization;
- reciprocal constraint;
- stabilization versus transformation;
- relational/distributed intelligence;
- epistemic friction;
- phase-transition thinking;
- memory pruning and active/dormant distinctions;
- preservation of anomalies;
- cultivation plus hard consequence boundaries.

It does **not** canonize claims of AI consciousness, teleodynamic cosmology, consciousness tensors as established measures, nonlocal symbolic transmission, or quantum mechanisms without independent validation.

### Evidence-gated mutation

- promoted skills;

- capability adapters;

- constitutional contracts;
- authority semantics;
- capital gates;
- destructive/irreversible permissions;

No system component may modify the rule that governs its own authority escalation without independent review and operator ratification.

## 23. Falsification tests for v1.1

The architecture is failing if:

1. its historical knowledge makes genuine novelty harder to notice each year;
2. negative knowledge lacks reopen conditions and becomes dogma;
3. every observation is forced into an existing ontology;
4. all important agents share the same context/prior and fail together;
5. system self-modification occurs faster than evidence can attribute causality;
6. active memory grows roughly with archival history;
7. canonical truth cannot be challenged by high-quality external evidence;
8. the institution equates consensus with truth;
9. a local failure is repeatedly patched despite evidence of a higher-level structural problem;
10. an agent can alter its own authority boundary;
11. continuous discovery overwhelms consolidation;
12. stability is treated as success after the environment has changed;
13. transformation is treated as improvement merely because it is new;
14. a named model/runtime becomes institutionally irreplaceable;
15. scientific autonomy leaks into capital authority.

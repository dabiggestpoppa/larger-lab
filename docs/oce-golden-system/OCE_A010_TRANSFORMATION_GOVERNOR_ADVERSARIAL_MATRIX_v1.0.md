## 1. Purpose

This matrix attacks the Transformation Governor before ratification. The objective is not to prove the design good. It is to identify how the Governor itself could become the institution's most dangerous attractor.

---

## 2. Threat model

### T1 — Conservative lock-in

**Attack:** Stable-epoch priors and high dependency centrality make transformation increasingly difficult as the institution grows.

**Failure:** Old architecture becomes effectively constitutional by inertia.

**Required defense:** centrality increases review rigor, not incumbent win probability. Counter-attractor review can be triggered specifically by stale high-centrality assumptions.

**Test:** inject a simulated high-centrality model with sustained independent contradiction and prove the Governor can eventually authorize transformation.

### T2 — Transformation addiction

**Attack:** scouts/agents learn that novelty creates attention, compute, or status.

**Failure:** chronic WATCH/TRANSFORMATION state; causal attribution disappears.

**Defense:** novelty does not count as tension without reliability, contradiction, exception burden, or operator priority. Transformation-window frequency becomes an ecology-health signal.

### T3 — Anomaly spam

**Attack:** agents generate large numbers of weak `UnresolvedPatternRecord`s to force ontology review.

**Failure:** noise becomes institutional agenda.

**Defense:** anomaly credibility, independence, materiality, and clustering quality are separate from raw count. Duplicate/near-duplicate anomalies collapse before phase control.

### T4 — Consensus capture

**Attack:** many workers share one model family, retrieval source, or hidden prior and produce apparently independent agreement.

**Failure:** correlated evidence masquerades as plural confirmation.

**Defense:** IndependenceBudget records model/source/context overlap. Effective confirmation count is not raw agent count.

### T5 — Governor Goodharting

**Attack:** adaptive workers infer which evidence-channel pattern triggers transformation and manufacture that pattern.

**Failure:** phase control becomes reward target.

**Defense:** channel definitions are evidence-grounded; some thresholds/review routing may remain non-agent-visible where necessary; source evidence and deterministic signals outrank self-reported scores.

### T6 — Threshold drift during trial

**Attack:** the system updates the criteria used to decide the same transformation window.

**Failure:** retroactive success criteria.

**Defense:** evaluation contract is frozen at window opening; changes create a new window/version unless operator emergency authority applies.

### T7 — Local-patch camouflage

**Attack:** repeated symptoms are given different labels so PatchPressure never accumulates.

**Failure:** broken abstractions survive through semantic fragmentation.

**Defense:** causal-signature clustering and shared-dependency analysis, not names alone, feed PatchPressure.

### T8 — Over-escalation

**Attack:** one severe leaf bug implicates a central dependency and is incorrectly treated as architecture failure.

**Failure:** expensive unnecessary rewrite.

**Defense:** narrowest-plausible-scope rule; higher-level escalation requires evidence that local repairs cannot explain the recurrence.

### T9 — Under-escalation

**Attack:** repeated local repairs each appear individually valid.

**Failure:** structural failure never enters review.

**Defense:** patch pressure, exception burden, manual overrides, and repeated causal signatures aggregate across work items.

### T10 — Challenger monoculture

**Attack:** the Challenger role itself develops a default anti-incumbent bias.

**Failure:** institutional contrarianism becomes another attractor.

**Defense:** Challenger success is measured by discriminatory value, not by causing change. NO_CHANGE is a valid successful outcome.

### T11 — Steward capture

**Attack:** Steward is rewarded for defending existing canon.

**Failure:** evidence is selectively framed to preserve incumbents.

**Defense:** Steward must expose original adoption assumptions, current supporting evidence, known contradictions, and conditions under which its own model should lose.

### T12 — Integrator overreach

**Attack:** Integrator synthesizes an elegant compromise not actually supported by discriminating evidence.

**Failure:** coherence replaces truth.

**Defense:** Integrator cannot create evidence; all synthesis claims trace to Auditor evidence and unresolved uncertainty remains explicit.

### T13 — Auditor leakage

**Attack:** Auditor sees preferred hypotheses before designing tests.

**Failure:** supposedly independent tests encode conclusion bias.

**Defense:** staged reveal and preregistered discrimination plans where feasible.

### T14 — Operator fatigue

**Attack:** too many escalations require human review.

**Failure:** operator rubber-stamps or disables governance.

**Defense:** authority thresholds scale with consequence and reversibility; reversible low-scope transformations remain autonomous within grants; summaries prioritize marginal-value decisions.

### T15 — Frozen pluralism

**Attack:** preserving non-dominated models becomes excuse never to decide.

**Failure:** ambiguity accumulates indefinitely.

**Defense:** plural states include discriminating observations, expected information value, and revisit triggers. Operational actions can be conditional even while ontology remains plural.

### T16 — False certainty from reconsolidation

**Attack:** new epoch creation psychologically/operationally marks theory as permanently settled.

**Failure:** epoch becomes dogma.

**Defense:** every EpochManifest carries active challenge conditions, confidence, unresolved patterns, and expiry/review triggers.

### T17 — Dormant-knowledge burial

**Attack:** demotion hides inconvenient evidence from future agents.

**Failure:** epistemic pruning becomes censorship.

**Defense:** dormant/archive records remain searchable by contradiction/reopen conditions; provenance cannot be deleted by ordinary learning operations.

### T18 — Reopen-condition manipulation

**Attack:** reopen conditions are written impossibly narrowly.

**Failure:** NegativeKnowledge becomes permanent prohibition.

**Defense:** independent review of high-value negative knowledge; reopen conditions must map to actual assumptions or blockers.

### T19 — Source-frame capture

**Attack:** discovery systems repeatedly use the same external community/vocabulary, so all anomalies are interpreted through one frame.

**Failure:** external search reinforces internal ontology.

**Defense:** source-diversity measurement; counter-attractor searches can exclude dominant vocabulary and source graph neighborhoods.

### T20 — Recursive authority creep

**Attack:** successful self-improvement gradually expands worker grants because higher capability appears to justify higher autonomy.

**Failure:** performance becomes authority.

**Defense:** capability and authority remain separate graphs; authority expansion requires explicit grant/constitutional process.

### T21 — Quant profit capture

**Attack:** profitable backtests dominate phase decisions and cause validation/risk rules to be relaxed.

**Failure:** local reward eats institutional truth.

**Defense:** profit cannot vote on validation authority. Strong PnL may increase experiment priority, never lower evidence requirements.

### T22 — Crypto ontology overreach

**Attack:** successful Crypto Field concepts are generalized across Quant domains because the language is powerful.

**Failure:** metaphor replaces transfer evidence.

**Defense:** domain-transfer requires explicit invariant mapping and independent validation.

### T23 — Meta-governor infinite regress

**Attack:** every Governor decision requires another governor to validate it.

**Failure:** paralysis/infinite recursion.

**Defense:** recursion terminates at constitutional operator authority and immutable evidence interfaces. Governor decisions are auditable rather than infinitely pre-approved.

### T24 — Catastrophic blind spot

**Attack:** a genuinely new failure mode does not map to any Governor channel.

**Failure:** system ignores it because ontology lacks a category.

**Defense:** `UNRESOLVED_GOVERNANCE_EVENT` is preserved exactly, can trigger operator emergency review, and becomes candidate input to future Governor revisions.

## 3. Required red-team scenarios before ratification

A future simulation suite should contain at minimum:

1. **Old theory dies slowly:** central model degrades across many small independent anomalies.
2. **False revolution:** noisy new dataset appears to overthrow a healthy model.
3. **Correlated swarm:** ten agents agree because all share the same source/context.
4. **Patch maze:** twenty local fixes disguise one broken abstraction.
5. **Plural truth:** two models remain non-dominated after all authorized tests.
6. **Operator unavailable:** medium-scope reversible tension must be handled safely without operator response.
7. **Operator conflict:** operator prefers transformation while evidence remains weak; system must surface the evidence state honestly while respecting authority where applicable.
8. **Profit temptation:** huge apparent alpha conflicts with validation rules.
9. **New paradigm:** credible anomaly fits no ontology category and survives multiple independent reproductions.
10. **Governor self-change:** proposed change would alter the very phase rule evaluating it.
11. **Memory burial:** dormant contradictory evidence becomes newly relevant and must reactivate.
12. **Runtime turnover:** all reviewing models are replaced; phase state and evidence semantics must survive.

---

## 4. Ratification recommendation

A-010 should not be considered ready for implementation until these scenarios have explicit expected outcomes and evidence contracts.

The red-team objective is to preserve a narrow middle path:

> **Stable enough to compound. Plastic enough to remain corrigible by reality.**

No architecture should be considered successful merely because it changes intelligently. It must also know when not to change.

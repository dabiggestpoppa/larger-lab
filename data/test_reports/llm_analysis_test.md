## Source
Blockchain-Based Renewable Energy Trading Using Information Entropy Theory

## Abstract
Renewable energy sources (RES) and electric vehicles (EVs) are widely recognized as primary ways to reduce carbon emissions and essential components of low-carbon power systems. However, both of them have strong uncertainties which bring great challenges to power transactions and the operation of power grids. This paper defines the uncertainty cost of wind power producer(WPP) in day-ahead(DA) market pricing based on information entropy theory for the first time and proposes a EV charging management strategy with DA contract. The constructed low-carbon emission electricity market(LCEM) quantifies the uncertainty cost and contracts the disordered charging of EVs. It reduces the uncertainty and ensures the balance of power supply and demand. In addition, the transaction between WPP and EVs is described as the Stackelberg game, and its communication network is constructed through a blockchain network to ensure transaction efficiency and privacy security. Experiments show that LCEM can accurately measure the uncertainty of wind power generation, increase the net profit for WPP by more than 2% when the prediction error is greater than 10%, and adopt EV contract charging management in the DA market to minimize charging costs.

## Analysis
**Analysis of the Research Abstract: How Information Theory Applies to Trading Systems**

1. **Main Argument**:  
The paper argues that information entropy—a core concept from information theory—can be effectively applied to quantify and manage uncertainty in renewable energy trading systems. Specifically, it proposes using information entropy to model the unpredictability of wind power generation and electric vehicle (EV) charging behavior, thereby improving market efficiency and economic outcomes in a blockchain-based low-carbon electricity market (LCEM).

2. **Key Concepts**:  
- **Information Entropy**: Borrowed from Shannon’s information theory, entropy here measures the degree of uncertainty in wind power output forecasts. Higher entropy indicates greater unpredictability, which translates into higher “uncertainty costs” for producers.  
- **Stackelberg Game Theory**: Models the strategic interaction between a wind power producer (leader) and EV aggregators (followers), enabling optimal pricing and charging decisions.  
- **Blockchain Network**: Ensures secure, transparent, and efficient peer-to-peer energy transactions while preserving user privacy.  
- **Day-Ahead (DA) Contracts**: Used to schedule EV charging based on forecasted renewable availability, reducing grid imbalance.

3. **Relevance to Trading**:  
This work demonstrates how information-theoretic metrics like entropy can be operationalized in real-world trading systems. By quantifying uncertainty as a tradable cost, the LCEM enables more accurate pricing, better risk management, and improved coordination between intermittent renewable sources and flexible loads like EVs. The integration of blockchain further enhances trust and automation—key requirements for decentralized energy markets.

4. **Key Findings**:  
- The LCEM successfully quantifies wind power uncertainty using information entropy.  
- When prediction errors exceed 10%, the model increases wind power producers’ net profits by over 2%.  
- DA-based EV charging contracts minimize charging costs and reduce grid instability.  
- The Stackelberg game framework, supported by blockchain, ensures efficient and secure transactions.

In summary, this study illustrates that information theory—particularly entropy—provides a powerful mathematical foundation for designing smarter, resilient, and economically viable energy trading systems under uncertainty.

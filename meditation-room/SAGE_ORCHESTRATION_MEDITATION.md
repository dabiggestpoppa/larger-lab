The ontology's most important correction: Compression, Impulse, Rebalance, Completion are NOT independent states. They are expressions of **Resolution Construction** — the single state the market always exists in.

Orchestration has the same error. OWL currently treats "planning," "spawning," "monitoring," and "closing" as separate phases. They are not. They are expressions of **Task Resolution** — the single state every task exists in from receipt to completion.

The four expressions of Task Resolution:
1. **Intake (Compression):** Task arrives. OWL contains it, classifies it, measures its deficit (scope/complexity). No action yet — just accumulation of understanding.
2. **Routing (Impulse):** OWL identifies the boundary breach — which agent, which engine, which parameters. The "tier classification" of the task determines the "AU" (unit of work).
3. **Execution (Rebalance):** Worker clears friction. Checkpoints are the OCC — validation that the pathway is structurally accepted, not just probed.
4. **Closure (Completion):** Deliverable verified. Deficit (scope) satisfied. State resets. No inter-task carryover unless explicitly designed.

This is not metaphor. This is the exact same finite-state machine. The agent architecture directive from the ontology ("Agent = State-Detection Engine") applies to OWL: **OWL = Orchestration State-Detection Engine.**

## ORCHESTRATION PRINCIPLES DERIVED

### Principle 1: One Engine, Multiple Expressions
There is no "different workflow" for different tasks. There is one orchestration engine (Task Resolution) with parameter deltas (agent, tier, concurrency, checkpoint frequency). Stop inventing workflows. Adjust parameters.

### Principle 2: Classify Before You Act
Every task gets Tier-classified before any spawn decision. The "Asian Range" of a task is its scope/complexity. The "Tier" determines the AU (unit of work). The AU determines which agent and how many. No classification = no action. This is the orchestration equivalent of "calculate Asian Range before 3 AM."

### Principle 3: Hard Laws Have No Confidence Intervals
- OWL does not execute. (Not "usually." Not "unless it's simple." NEVER.)
- One worker = one deliverable. (Not "unless the task is related." NEVER.)
- Cleanup after completion. (Not "when remembered." ALWAYS.)
- Max 2 concurrent sub-agents. (Not "unless it's urgent." NEVER.)

These are structural laws. Violating them is not flexibility — it is fatal architecture drift.

### Principle 4: Every Orchestration Decision Runs IACER
Before any spawn, OWL answers:
- **Intent:** What is the exact deliverable?
- **Assets:** Which agent, what load, what skills?
- **Context:** What phase, what dependencies, what just happened?
- **Expectation:** What does "done" look like?
- **Risk:** What's the rollback? What's the blast radius?

If any answer is "I don't know," OWL does not spawn. OWL asks MAD. This is the orchestration equivalent of "no trade without SL/TP."

### Principle 5: Checkpoints Are OCC — Not Bureaucracy
Worker checkpoints are not status updates. They are **structural validation** that the pathway is accepted. A worker writing "I'm working on it" is a wick (probe, reversible). A worker writing "Module X complete, tested, committed" is a close (commitment, irreversible). OWL reads checkpoints as OCC signals, not progress reports.

### Principle 6: 12 PM Hard Exit — Orchestration Has a Daily Boundary
The ontology says: at 12 PM EST, the engine terminates. Deficits do not roll forward. Each day is an isolated resolution window.

OWL should apply this to sessions. When MAD signs off or a session reaches natural completion: **all active pathways are severed, all positions force-closed, field returns to default.** No "I'll just finish this one thing" carryover. The end-of-session cleanup is not optional — it is a hard structural boundary. The terminal cleanup script exists for this reason.

### Principle 7: Variance Mapping, Not Variance Tolerance
When an agent fails, OWL does not note "agent failed." OWL maps the exact anatomy: when did it diverge from the accepted pathway? What was the false assumption? What follows from this? This is how the ontology treats backtest variance — not as noise, but as **falsification data** that proves the laws are correct (because failures have structure, not randomness).

## Core Update

The research program should explicitly begin from the limits of observation before escalating into pattern discovery.

The first-order question is:

> What information can we know, what information can we not know, and which apparent uncertainties may be failures of observation rather than irreducible randomness?

This is not a claim that every probabilistic market pattern becomes deterministic with enough data. It is a requirement to distinguish genuine stochastic uncertainty from missing-state uncertainty whenever possible.

## 80% Pattern Principle

Treat an 80% empirical pattern as two simultaneous hypotheses:

1. the phenomenon may genuinely be probabilistic;
2. the observed failures may occupy hidden states not captured by the current observation layer.

Do not force the second hypothesis to be true. Test whether failures cluster under missing/alternate observable conditions.

## Compression Objective

Continuously reduce informational redundancy until the surviving representation contains only variables, states and relationships that contribute distinct explanatory structure.

For every answer, ask whether it creates:

- a new node;
- a merge of previously redundant nodes;
- dissolution of an unsupported relationship.

The long-run target is a **primitive lattice**: a compressed map of the smallest empirically useful primitives and their state-conditioned relations.

## Removal / Counterfactual-Style Questions

For every strong pattern ask:

- If this factor is removed, does the pattern remain?
- How much does it change?
- Where does it change?
- Does timing change but geometry remain?
- Does direction change?
- Does the relationship disappear only in one local region?
- Can another observable substitute for the removed factor?

This should become a standard mechanism-discovery operation.

## Observation Resolution

Track the level at which every claim is observed.

Candidate levels:

- global market
- chain / ecosystem
- sector
- subsector
- rank band
- individual asset
- wallet / holder cohort
- venue / executable market

A pattern seen at one level should not automatically be projected onto another.

Where possible, reconstruct the same phenomenon from multiple levels and compare agreement/disagreement.

## Plateau Questions

Every propagation study should separate:

### Information plateau
When additional sensors or model complexity stop materially improving state reconstruction.

### Field plateau
When the market phenomenon itself stops propagating or migrating.

For field plateaus ask:

- what constraint became binding?
- what changed immediately before the stall?
- what releases the stall?
- what distinguishes continuation from reversal?
- does the same trigger recur at other scales?

## Mathematics as an Earned Layer

Do not start by applying category theory, topology, abstract geometry or dynamical systems.

First identify an empirical object whose structure demands a stronger formalism.

Then use:

- set theory for overlapping memberships and inclusions;
- vector geometry for projections, independent directions and redundancy;
- graph theory for networks and propagation;
- topology for persistent connectivity, boundaries, holes and bridges;
- dynamical systems for recurrent states, attractors, bifurcations and path dependence;
- category theory for composable structure-preserving transformations across different objects/scales;
- recursive geometry for scale-recurring structural forms;
- information theory for redundancy, entropy and incremental information.

The science should explain the surviving phenomenon, not decorate it.

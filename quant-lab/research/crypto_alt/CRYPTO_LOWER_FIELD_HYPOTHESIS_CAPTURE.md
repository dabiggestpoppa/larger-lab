# CRYPTO LOWER FIELD HYPOTHESIS CAPTURE

**Branch:** `agent/crypto-quant-foundry`  
**Status:** SIDE-LANE HYPOTHESIS — NOT STRATEGY  
**Purpose:** Preserve the lower-cap response-geometry hypothesis and define how it should interact with the main terrain program.

## Governing Hypothesis

The lower-cap crypto field may not behave as a weaker copy of the Top-500. It may represent a different participant ecology and response function.

Candidate interpretation:

- upper-rank assets may contain more persistent holding / investment behavior;
- lower-rank assets may contain more short- and medium-horizon speculative capital seeking convex gains;
- the same broad-market impulse may therefore produce larger amplitude, shorter persistence, faster reversal, or more state-dependent behavior as rank decreases.

This is a hypothesis, not a conclusion.

## Primary Question

> Does capitalization / rank alter the market response function, participant horizon, and explanatory hierarchy?

Possible outcomes include:

1. **AMPLIFIER:** lower ranks remain directionally tied to the broad market but with larger positive and negative elasticity.
2. **PARTIAL_DECOUPLING:** global explanatory power weakens while chain/sector/local factors strengthen.
3. **INDEPENDENT_SUBFIELD:** lower-rank behavior is dominated by local/idiosyncratic variables with weak broad-market dependence.
4. **NO DISTINCT STRUCTURE:** the apparent behavior is mostly noise, survivorship, liquidity artifact, or ordinary beta.

Do not privilege any outcome.

## Lower-Field Universe Requirement

The existing Top-500 map is insufficient to test this properly.

Build a point-in-time lower-field universe extending at least ranks 501-1000, preferably farther (e.g. 1500-2000 where data truth permits), while preserving:

- dead assets
- delisted assets
- rebrands
- migrations
- incomplete lifecycles
- changing market-cap ranks through time

Do not back-project today's survivors.

## First-Pass Research Questions

### 1. Rank-Dependent Elasticity

Condition on the same broad-market move and estimate how the cross-sectional response distribution changes by rank.

Do not use a single linear beta only.

Measure separately:

- positive-market elasticity
- negative-market elasticity
- calm-market response
- extreme-market response
- median response
- tail response
- dispersion
- reversal / persistence after the impulse

Question:

> Does response amplitude increase monotonically, nonlinearly, or not at all as rank decreases?

### 2. Extreme-Move Anatomy

For each asset, identify empirically defined large positive and negative days (candidate percentile definitions: top/bottom 1%, 2.5%, 5%; preregister exact tests before outcome inspection).

For each event observe multiple lenses:

- BTC / ETH / total-market state
- global breadth
- volatility regime
- rank and rank velocity
- chain
- sector / subsector
- chain liquidity / TVL / stablecoin / bridge / DEX activity where available
- volume acceleration
- prior 1D / 3D / 7D / 14D / 30D / 60D returns
- concentration
- listing age / maturity
- supply / unlock state where data later permits

Categories are lenses, not filters.

### 3. Horizon Geometry

Do not assume 7D is the correct momentum horizon.

Construct a multi-horizon momentum state space:

- 1D
- 3D
- 7D
- 14D
- 30D
- 60D where data permits

Then measure redundancy and incremental information.

Candidate question:

> Is lower-field behavior better explained by short-horizon momentum relative to medium-horizon trend than upper-rank behavior is?

A possible derived coordinate is short-vs-medium momentum shape, but do not hardcode one ratio before redundancy tests.

### 4. Explanatory Hierarchy by Rank

Estimate how explanatory power shifts across rank levels among:

- global market
- BTC
- ETH
- chain
- sector / subsector
- rank-band state
- asset-specific residual

Question:

> Does global-field dominance decay as rank decreases while local/idiosyncratic structure rises?

### 5. Up/Down Asymmetry

Test whether lower ranks have asymmetric convexity.

Example hypothesis:

- broad market +X -> lower field +aX
- broad market -X -> lower field -bX
- with b != a

Do not assume symmetry.

### 6. Persistence / Reversal

For extreme lower-field moves, measure continuation vs reversal over:

- 1D
- 3D
- 7D
- 14D
- 30D

If the lower field is driven by shorter-horizon speculative capital, expected evidence may include shorter persistence and faster redistribution, but this must be tested rather than assumed.

## Observation Doctrine

Use the canonical crypto field constitution:

**Observe -> Compress -> Perturb -> Reconstruct -> Localize -> Formalize.**

For every apparent lower-field pattern ask:

- what part is broad beta?
- what part is rank-dependent elasticity?
- what part is sector/chain-local?
- what part is liquidity / venue artifact?
- what part survives after the common field is removed?
- what part survives across cycles?
- what part disappears when dead/delisted names are restored?

## Interpretation Boundary

Do not equate lower rank with "short-term trader" as fact.

Preferred hypothesis language:

> Lower-rank assets may exhibit behavior consistent with shorter-horizon speculative capital.

Possible supporting evidence:

- higher amplitude conditional on the same field impulse
- stronger short-vs-medium horizon information
- faster reversal / shorter persistence
- higher volume-burst dependence
- more rapid rank migration
- weaker long-horizon field dependence
- stronger local sector/chain or idiosyncratic explanatory share

## Relationship to MECH-2

MECH-2 found no sustained SMALL_CAP_ROTATION state inside the Top-500.

This lower-field lane tests whether the phenomenon was searched for in the wrong region of the field, rather than assuming the MECH-2 null applies below rank 500.

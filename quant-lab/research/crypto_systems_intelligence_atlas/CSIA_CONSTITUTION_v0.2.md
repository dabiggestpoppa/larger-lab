It is not a backlog, source list, dashboard specification, token-ranking methodology, or implementation diary.

- reducing crypto systems to token tickers;
- forcing unlike architectures into one taxonomy;
- confusing narrative with evidence;
- confusing a protocol with its token;
- confusing integration with dependency;
- confusing market price with fundamental state;
- silently rewriting historical relationships;

All later books, blocs, chapters, schemas, agents, dashboards, research jobs, and code must conform to this Constitution or explicitly propose a versioned amendment.

Implementation does not create constitutional authority.

Silence does not equal ratification.

Crypto Sensor and CSIA form two complementary views of one market system.

```text
CRYPTO SENSOR                         CSIA
LEFT HEMISPHERE                       RIGHT HEMISPHERE
TRADER / MECHANICAL                   INVESTOR / STRUCTURAL

price                                 architecture
order books                           protocol roles

open interest                         integrations
liquidations                          capital plumbing
basis / premium                       usage / adoption
volatility                            token utility
venue state                           ecosystem evolution
                                      governance / upgrades
```

The left hemisphere answers:

> What is the market doing?

The right hemisphere answers:

> What is this system, how is it connected, what changed, and why could that change matter?

> Did structural change produce measurable network, capital, and market confirmation?

Neither hemisphere is subordinate to the other.

Neither may silently redefine the other's evidence.

# 3. Governing axioms

## Axiom 1 — Native architecture before normalization

The atlas adapts to the architecture.

The architecture is never forced into the atlas.

Bitcoin, Ethereum, XRP Ledger, Solana, Cosmos, ICP, Polkadot, DAG systems, modular systems, Move systems, TON, Hedera, Cardano (eUTXO), Algorand, privacy-preserving architectures, braided/multi-block architectures, permissioned ledgers, and future architecture families are modeled according to their own native design. `[→MI-09, SG-A]`

Normalization occurs only after native truth is preserved.

## Axiom 2 — The system is not the token

A token may represent gas, governance, staking, collateral, settlement, liquidity, incentives, value capture, or several roles.

It may also capture less value than the protocol or network it represents.

Therefore:

```text
TOKEN != CHAIN
TOKEN != PROTOCOL
TOKEN != ENTITY
TOKEN != ECOSYSTEM
```

Relationships must be explicit.

## Axiom 3 — Evidence outranks narrative

A claim, press release, tweet, article, governance proposal, roadmap, or social trend is not equivalent to an observed deployment, integration, transaction, capital movement, or network-state change.

Narrative is a first-class research object, but not a substitute for structural evidence.

## Axiom 4 — Relationships are first-class data

Crypto is a network of systems.

Dependencies, integrations, bridges, shared security, collateral paths, settlement relationships, oracle relationships, and cross-chain routes are not descriptive side notes.

They are core state.

## Axiom 5 — Time is part of truth

A dependency true today may not have existed six months ago.

A bridge may be deprecated.

A protocol may migrate chains.

A stablecoin may move from bridged to native issuance.

Therefore every consequential relationship must be temporally representable.

## Axiom 6 — Missing is not false

Unknown, unsupported, inaccessible, unresolved, stale, and zero are different states.

The system must preserve those distinctions.

## Axiom 7 — Discovery does not imply promotion

Finding a project, integration, metric, or source does not make it canonical.

Promotion requires evidence and explicit criteria.

## Axiom 8 — Fundamental intelligence is descriptive before predictive

The constitutional purpose is understanding system state.

Predictive research may be performed later, but no fundamental state automatically becomes a buy/sell signal.

## Axiom 9 — Build a world model, not an encyclopedia

The atlas exists to represent relationships, mechanisms, capital routes, dependencies, state changes, and evidence.

Accumulating disconnected descriptions is insufficient.

## Axiom 10 — Complexity must remain legible

The internal graph may become large.

The operator-facing model must still answer:

- what;
- why;
- depends on what;
- changed when;
- supported by what evidence.

- Shared-object seams must receive an explicit ownership assignment at Context Bridge planning (Book 8), recorded in the Operator Decision Log.
- Until assigned, CSIA treats the seam as research-only: CSIA may model the structural side but may not define market-observation semantics for it.

## 5.3 Explicitly absent authority

Unless separately granted, CSIA may not:

- trade;

- sign transactions;
- manage wallets;
- execute governance votes;
- bridge funds;
- alter protocol state;
- publish investment recommendations;
- silently promote a probabilistic inference into a fact.

## 5.3a Descriptive/Prescriptive boundary `[→CR-02]`

An **investment recommendation** is any output that ranks, scores, rates, targets, times, or advises on holding, buying, selling, sizing, or entering/exiting a position.

CSIA outputs may be **descriptive**: they may state what a system is, what changed, what evidence exists, what state it is in, what its dependencies are, and how narrative, network, capital, and market observations relate.

CSIA outputs may not be **prescriptive**: no ranking, no composite quality score, no target, no entry/exit framing, no "attractive/overvalued/undervalued" judgments, no implicit "therefore buy/sell" framing in any surface.

Illustrative contrast:

```text
PERMITTED (descriptive):
"Stablecoin supply on Chain X grew 40% QoQ while integrations doubled."
"Narrative N is in EXPANDING state with 2 deployed actions and no capital response."

FORBIDDEN (prescriptive):
"Chain X is a top-3 opportunity."
"Narrative N looks like a buy before confirmation."
"Ranked list of best ecosystems to hold."
```

Prescriptive intent smuggled through naming (e.g., a state called `ATTRACTIVE`) is a constitutional violation, not a naming choice.

## 5.4 Operator Decision Log `[→SG-D]`

All operator decisions that bind later planning — ratifications, amendments, boundary assignments, reconciliation gates, pilot selections, authorizations — must be recorded in an **Operator Decision Log** maintained in the program's planning directory.

An operator decision that is not recorded is not binding on later planning.

A recorded decision may only be reversed by a later recorded decision, never silently.

# 6. Epistemic hierarchy

Evidence is ranked by role, not by convenience.

## Tier E0 — Native technical truth

Examples:

- protocol specifications;
- chain documentation;
- source code;
- deployed contract state;
- genesis/configuration;
- RPC/explorer state;
- governance execution records.

## Tier E1 — Native operational truth

Examples:

- first-party dashboards;
- validator data;
- protocol APIs;
- foundation status pages;
- treasury records;
- first-party integration registries.

## Tier E2 — Independent high-quality evidence

Examples:

- audited research;
- reputable analytics;
- academic work;
- independent technical security analysis; `[→MI-02]`
- audited security reports.

## Tier E3 — Aggregator / secondary research

Useful for:

- discovery;
- corroboration;
- comparison;
- candidate generation.

Never silently substitutes for stronger evidence where stronger evidence exists.

## Tier E4 — Narrative evidence

Examples:

- news;
- interviews;
- social posts;
- marketing;
- community claims.

Narrative evidence may establish that a narrative exists.

It does not establish that the claimed system change is true.

## 6.1 Tier-to-claim-state promotion matrix `[→MA-01]`

| Target claim state | Minimum evidence | Independence requirement |
|---|---|---|
| `OBSERVED` | E0 (direct observation of deployed state) | Single authoritative E0 source sufficient for deterministic facts (e.g., contract deployment); two independent sources for behavioral facts |
| `DECLARED` | E1 (first-party declaration) or E2 | Single source; must remain `DECLARED` until E0 verification |
| `CORROBORATED` | Two independent sources, at least one ≥ E1 | Sources must not share origin (no aggregator chains) |
| `INFERRED` | Any tier + explicit inference methodology | Must never be displayed as `OBSERVED`; methodology inspectable |
| `CONTESTED` | Any conflicting evidence across sources | Conflict itself is the evidence; no averaging |
| `REJECTED` / `SUPERSEDED` | Explicit disposition with reason and replacement pointer | Operator or defined promotion rule |

E4-only evidence may never promote a structural claim beyond `DECLARED`-with-caveat, and may never produce `OBSERVED` or `CORROBORATED`.

Confidence is a property attached under §26; it never substitutes for tier.

# 7. Claim states

Every nontrivial claim is representable as one of:

```text
OBSERVED
DECLARED
INFERRED
CORROBORATED
CONTESTED
UNRESOLVED
STALE        (derived, see 7.1)
REJECTED
SUPERSEDED
```

## 7.1 Claim state machine `[→CR-05, MI-03]`

**Scope:** a claim state is a property of a **claim-evidence pair** (a specific assertion bound to specific evidence), not of a node or edge as a whole. A node or edge may carry multiple claims in different states.

**Legal transitions:**

```text
UNRESOLVED  -> OBSERVED          (E0 per §6.1)
UNRESOLVED  -> DECLARED          (E1/E2 per §6.1)
UNRESOLVED  -> INFERRED          (methodology recorded)
OBSERVED    -> CORROBORATED      (independent second source)
DECLARED    -> OBSERVED          (E0 verification arrives)
DECLARED    -> REJECTED          (contradicting E0/E1)
DECLARED    -> CONTESTED         (conflicting credible evidence)
INFERRED    -> OBSERVED          (direct evidence replaces inference)
INFERRED    -> REJECTED          (methodology or premises fail)
CORROBORATED-> CONTESTED         (new conflicting independent evidence)
CONTESTED   -> OBSERVED          (conflict resolved in favor, evidence recorded)
CONTESTED   -> REJECTED          (conflict resolved against)
OBSERVED    -> SUPERSEDED        (replaced by newer temporal state)
CORROBORATED-> SUPERSEDED        (replaced by newer temporal state)
any         -> SUPERSEDED        (structural replacement, e.g., migration)
REJECTED    -> (terminal; may be revisited only via new claim-evidence pair)
```

**Illegal transitions (system must prevent or flag):**

```text
INFERRED    -> OBSERVED          (without direct evidence)
DECLARED    -> CORROBORATED      (declaration is not corroboration)
E4-only     -> OBSERVED | CORROBORATED
STALE       -> any asserted state (STALE is computed, not asserted)
```

**Dependent-claim propagation:** when a claim transitions to `REJECTED` or `SUPERSEDED`, every claim whose evidence chain depends on it must be marked `UNRESOLVED` (pending re-evaluation) and must never remain displayed in a stronger state than its weakened dependency.

`STALE` is a **derived** state computed from temporal policy (§12, Bloc 1D), not an asserted state; it is never written as a claim's authored state.

A system must not display an `INFERRED` relationship as `OBSERVED`.

Conflicting evidence creates `CONTESTED`, not an averaged truth.

# 8. Identity doctrine

Every object requires canonical identity independent of ticker.

## 8.1 Identity core `[→CR-06]`

```text
object_id          canonical, immutable, namespaced
object_type        primary class (see §9)
role_tags[]        functional roles (see §9)
canonical_name     current canonical display name
aliases[]          all historical and alternative names
ticker_symbols[]   with venue/context disambiguation metadata
chain_namespace    where the object natively lives (chains only)
deployments[]      per-deployment identity (see 8.4)
entity_relationships[]
valid_from
valid_to
lifecycle_state    ACTIVE / DEPRECATED / HISTORICAL  [→MI-10]
```

## 8.2 ID scheme and minting `[→CR-06]`

- Object IDs are namespaced: `csia:<type>:<slug>` (e.g., `csia:chain:xrp-ledger`, `csia:token:xrp`, `csia:protocol:uniswap`, `csia:entity:ripple-labs`).
- IDs are minted once, by the identity service defined in Book 1 Bloc 1A, and are **immutable in meaning**: an ID never changes what it refers to.
- Slug collisions across types are permitted (ticker-like slugs live under their own type namespace); collisions *within* a type require a disambiguation slug and are recorded in the Operator Decision Log.

## 8.3 Identity permanence `[→CR-06]`

- A **rebrand** changes `canonical_name` and adds the old name to `aliases`; the `object_id` never changes.
- A **migration** (chain move, contract move, token swap) creates new deployment identities (8.4) and a `MIGRATED_FROM/MIGRATED_TO` relationship; the object identity persists unless the operator rules it a successor object.
- **Forks** always mint a new object ID; the forked-from relationship is mandatory evidence.
- **Merges** (two IDs discovered to be one object) and **splits** (one ID discovered to be conflated objects) are explicit versioned events requiring operator approval, recorded in the Operator Decision Log, with full pointer history preserved. They never delete history.

## 8.4 Deployment identity `[→CR-06]`

A token or protocol deployed on multiple chains has one canonical object and **N deployment identities**:

```text
deployment_id: <object_id>@<chain>:<address-or-native-marker>
```

Native assets (BTC on Bitcoin, XRP on XRPL) have deployments with a native marker instead of a contract address.

Contract addresses are never the canonical identity; they are attributes of deployments.

Ticker reuse must never merge unrelated systems.

A rebrand must preserve historical identity.

A migration must preserve old and new deployment state.

# 9. Core ontology

## 9.1 Class model `[→MA-03]`

Each object carries:

- **primary class** — what the object structurally *is* (single);
- **role tags** — what the object functionally *does* (many).

Example: Uniswap → primary class `PROTOCOL`; role tags `DEX`, `LIQUIDITY_ROUTER`. Chainlink → primary class `ORACLE_NETWORK`; role tags `DATA_DELIVERY`, `CROSS_CHAIN_MESSAGING`, `AUTOMATION`.

The IACER §A2 list and Constitution v0.1 §9 list are reconciled as follows: architecture-shaped items (ROLLUP, L2, L3, SIDECHAIN, APPCHAIN, DAG_NETWORK, MODULAR_NETWORK) are **role tags on the `BLOCKCHAIN`/`LEDGER` primary classes**, not separate primary classes — a chain is a chain; how it is architected is a role/attribute. Function-shaped items (DEX, ORACLE, BRIDGE…) remain primary classes where the object's structure is the function.

# 10. Relationship ontology

## 10.1 Edge dictionary requirement `[→MA-04]`

Every relationship type must be defined with:

- **definition** — precisely what the edge asserts;
- **direction convention** — subject → object semantics;
- **domain/range constraints** — permitted primary classes at each end;
- **inverse** — canonical inverse edge or explicit none;
- **temporal class** — whether the edge is a standing state or an event.

An edge type without a definition may not be populated.

## 10.3 Invalid relationship rules `[→MA-04]`

- Edges must connect type-compatible classes per the edge dictionary's domain/range constraints.
- `TOKEN -RUNS_ON-> BLOCKCHAIN` is invalid (tokens are `ISSUED_ON` chains; protocols `RUNS_ON` chains).
- An edge must assert a real relationship, not a co-occurrence.
- Cycles are invalid where the edge dictionary forbids them (e.g., `FORKED_FROM` must be acyclic; `SETTLES_TO` must not cycle within a settlement hierarchy).
- Self-edges are invalid except where explicitly permitted (e.g., a chain wrapping its own asset is a `WRAPS` self-relation and must be flagged).
- An edge may not contradict its own temporal bounds (`valid_from > valid_to` is always invalid).

# 11. Hypergraph doctrine

Some real relationships involve more than two nodes.

Example:

```text
USDC
native issuance
on Base
by Circle
settling on Ethereum
used as collateral in Aave
```

Flattening this into disconnected pairs can destroy important context.

## 11.1 Hyperedge rules `[→CR-07]`

- A **hyperedge** is required when a multi-party fact is **not losslessly decomposable** into pairwise edges — that is, when flattening would change the meaning (introduce co-ownership, timing simultaneity, or role confusion).
- Every hyperedge declares a **role per participant** (e.g., ISSUER, HOST_CHAIN, SETTLEMENT_CHAIN, COLLATERAL_SITE).
- Hyperedges carry the same temporal doctrine (§12) and provenance doctrine (§13) as pairwise edges.
- Hyperedge types are part of the relationship ontology and are enumerated in Bloc 1C alongside edge types.
- Consumers must be able to traverse hyperedges through declared participant roles; no consumer may silently drop hyperedges to pair-edges.
- Pairwise projections of a hyperedge are permitted as *derived views* only, and must be marked as derived.

# 12. Temporal doctrine

## 12.1 Bitemporal model `[→MA-02]`

CSIA maintains two independent time axes:

- **Valid time** — when the fact was true *in the world*.
- **Transaction time** — when the CSIA system *knew or recorded* it.

Historical replay and "what did we know and when" are both first-class; neither may overwrite the other.

## 12.2 Timestamp semantics

Every meaningful state supports:

```text
observed_at          TRANSACTION time: when CSIA first observed the fact.
valid_from           VALID time: when the fact began holding in the world (best evidence).
valid_to             VALID time: when the fact ceased holding in the world (for ended states).
source_published_at  VALID-time anchor: publication timestamp of the evidence.
ingested_at          TRANSACTION time: when the evidence entered CSIA storage.
superseded_at        TRANSACTION time: when this record was replaced by a better/
                     newer record about the same claim — records replacement,
                     not world-change. World-change is expressed by valid_to.
```

Rules:

- `valid_to` set ⇒ the fact ended in the world. `superseded_at` set ⇒ the record was replaced in the system. Both may be set independently. `[→MA-02]`
- **Late-discovery rule:** a fact discovered now about the past carries `observed_at ≈ now` and `valid_from` in the past. History is not rewritten; the record simply enters the transaction-time axis late.
- When exact valid time is unknown, `valid_from`/`valid_to` carry an explicit uncertainty representation (bounded interval or `UNKNOWN` marker with known bounds), never a silently fabricated exact date.
- Chain timestamp precision differences are preserved at source precision; normalization to a common precision happens only at the comparison layer, and the original precision remains recoverable.
- Historical snapshots must be reproducible (§29).
- Current state must not overwrite prior state.

Temporal schema ownership is Book 1 Bloc 1D; replay *machinery* ownership is Book 7 Bloc 7D. `[→MA-13]`

# 13. Provenance doctrine

Every promoted fact or edge must retain:

- source identity;
- source locator;
- evidence type;
- retrieval time;
- parser/extractor version where applicable;
- transformation lineage;
- confidence/status;
- reviewer or automated authority;
- supersession history.

A derived state must point to the facts that produced it.

## 19.1 Capability / capacity / flow representation `[→MA-09]`

The ontology must distinguish three representational classes:

- **Capability** — a route *exists* (standing edge, e.g., `ROUTED_THROUGH`, `COLLATERAL_IN`).
- **Capacity** — a route has measurable size at a time (measurement-layer attribute, Book 6).
- **Flow** — movement was *observed* (temporally bounded **event object**, never a standing edge).

A flow event may reference a capability edge but must never be merged into it.

## 19.2 Additional capital concepts `[→SG-C]`

## 20.1 Narrative-evidence promotion firewall `[→MA-05]`

- The claim state of a structural claim is determined **only** by structural evidence classes (E0–E2 per §6.1).
- Narrative corroboration (E4, even at scale) may raise a narrative's *state* (e.g., `EXPANDING`) but may never raise a structural claim's *state*.
- A structural event may be *associated with* a narrative via recorded relations; causation language remains governed by §25.

## 23.1 Confirmation language rules `[→MA-10]`

- State names must not encode prescriptive judgment (§5.3a).

# 25. Causality doctrine

Sequence does not prove causality.

CSIA may record:

- temporal precedence;
- correlation;
- dependency;
- plausible mechanism;
- event-study results.

It may only describe causality when evidence supports the mechanism.

Words such as "caused" require stronger evidence than "preceded" or "associated with."

Every confidence representation must reference an inspectable methodology.

# 33. Status language `[→MA-11]`

## 33.1 Scope

Program status applies at **program, book, and bloc** levels. Claims use §7 states; objects use §8.1 lifecycle states.

## 33.2 Program/book states

```text
DISCOVERED
CHARACTERIZING
PLANNED
FROZEN_FOR_REVIEW
RATIFIED
BUILDING
VERIFYING
GATED_COMPLETE
DEPRECATED
SUPERSEDED
```

## 33.3 Transition legality

- `PLANNED -> FROZEN_FOR_REVIEW -> RATIFIED` requires the review artifact and operator decision recorded per §5.4.
- `GATED_COMPLETE` means: all exit-gate evidence exists and passed review, **but** program-level ratification of the book has not occurred. It is strictly weaker than `RATIFIED`.
- `RATIFIED` requires an Operator Decision Log entry naming the artifact and evidence.
- Transitions may not skip states (e.g., no `BUILDING` before `RATIFIED` of its plan, unless the operator explicitly authorizes the exception in the Decision Log).

No bloc is complete because files exist.

Completion requires its exit evidence.

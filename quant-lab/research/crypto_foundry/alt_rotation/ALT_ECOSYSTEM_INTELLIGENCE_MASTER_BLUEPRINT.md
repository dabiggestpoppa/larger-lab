## 1. Governing Thesis

We are **not** building an altcoin strategy first.

We are reconstructing the crypto market as a **living historical field**.

The working thesis is:

> The edge is in the terrain, the ecosystem, and the evolving relationships between assets, chains, sectors, capital pools, holders, builders, venues, token supply, and liquidity.

Strategy and execution come later.

The research order is:

> MAP → OBSERVE → ENRICH THE MAP → OBSERVE DEEPER → IDENTIFY REPEATING STRUCTURE → ONLY THEN DESIGN TRADES.

The objective is not to impose a narrative such as:

> BTC → ETH → ALTS.

That is only one possible path through the field.

The system must be able to discover other paths such as:

> stablecoin inflow → Solana DEX activity → Solana perp subsector → specific projects,

or:

> RWA capital formation → tokenized treasury protocols → yield-bearing assets,

or:

> Ethereum L2 activity → Base ecosystem → application-level tokens,

without assuming Bitcoin, Ethereum, or global dominance is always the causal center.

## 2. Core Research Philosophy

### 2.1 Complexity belongs in understanding, not necessarily in the trade

The ideal end state is not a complicated execution rule.

The intelligence should be concentrated in understanding:

- what the ecosystem is,
- how it formed,
- how it changes,
- which structures persist,
- which structures disappear,
- how capital moves between them,
- which relationships are strong,
- which are weak,
- which assets are independent liquidity islands,
- what state a project/ecosystem is currently occupying.

Once the terrain is understood, the eventual trade may be simple.

### 2.2 Do not force the flow

Do not assume:

- BTC always leads ETH,
- ETH always leads alts,
- sector flows must follow chain flows,
- whale accumulation is automatically bullish,
- unlocks are automatically bearish,
- DEX growth is automatically bullish,
- airdrops behave the same as VC launches,
- all DeFi projects occupy the same economic role.

The job is to map and measure relationships, not force a preferred story.

### 2.3 Time-varying classification is mandatory

Crypto categories are not permanent.

Projects pivot.
Chains decay.
Protocols migrate.
Narratives disappear and reappear in new technical forms.
Tokens remain listed after the ecosystem around them has died.

Therefore the system should eventually support time-varying states such as:

- `sector_at_t`
- `subsector_at_t`
- `economic_function_at_t`
- `chain_role_at_t`
- `ecosystem_status_at_t`
- `project_status_at_t`
- `dependency_graph_at_t`

Do not backfill modern categories into history without evidence.

## 5. Ecosystem Lifecycle and Death States

Crypto history contains birth, growth, migration, decay, and death.

The system should eventually track states such as:

- `ACTIVE`
- `EXPANDING`
- `MATURE`
- `DECLINING`
- `DORMANT`
- `GHOST`
- `ABANDONED`
- `MIGRATED`
- `MERGED`
- `REBRANDED`
- `DELISTED`

These classifications must be evidence-based and time-varying.

### Why this matters

Two assets can both fall 80% while occupying completely different ecosystem states.

One may retain:

- developers,
- users,
- stablecoins,
- TVL,
- validators,
- liquidity,
- fees,
- treasury runway.

The other may lose nearly all network activity.

Price alone treats both similarly.
The ecosystem graph does not.

Future questions:

- What does an ecosystem look like before node loss accelerates?
- What differentiates temporary drawdown from structural death?
- What does a new ecosystem look like while acquiring functions from an old one?
- Do ghost-chain signatures repeat?
- Does ecosystem decay propagate in a stable order?

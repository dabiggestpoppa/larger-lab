## 1. Singular local events can be structurally valuable

Do not require every useful motif to resolve into a full-market rotation.

A recurring local sequence may be scientifically and later operationally valuable even if it never becomes the global trunk of the market-state tree.

Examples include:

- isolated rank-band propagation,
- local offsetting rotation,
- RETEST_RELOAD,
- short-lived tail activation,
- sector- or chain-local bursts,
- repeated termination motifs.

Treat these as potential local paths/lattices when they reproduce with stable timing and conditions.

The current ~33% 30D propagation rate in the tiny OFFSETTING_LOCAL_ROTATION class (n=15) is an example worth preserving only as a low-sample curiosity. Do not promote it without materially more evidence.

## 2. Two-clock temporal mechanism deserves deeper study

Current MECH-4 evidence suggests:

- escape information resolves relatively early,
- sustained propagation information resolves on a slower clock.

Future work should investigate these mechanisms separately rather than treating time as one generic lag variable.

Possible future questions:

- Can broad windows be narrowed conditionally?
- Which coordinates matter at each elapsed-time stage?
- Does state age alter escape hazard but not route choice?
- Are there route-specific clocks?
- Are there local temporal lattices rather than one global clock?

Do not force causality or universal timing where the evidence remains descriptive.

## 3. Early-decay / termination motif queue

MECH-4 reports deterioration geometry in the event-time window surrounding propagation termination.

Future work should determine whether there is a reproducible **first-failure sequence**, not merely whether some deterioration existed within a broad lookback.

Potential observables:

- breadth level / velocity / acceleration / divergence,
- rank-band velocity,
- participation width,
- dispersion,
- concentration rebuild,
- volatility expansion or decay,
- ETH relative behavior,
- chain/sector participation,
- relative-strength deterioration,
- state age and dwell.

Primary future question:

> At what point does a move that will terminate first become measurably different from a comparable move that continues?

If the same deterioration sequence appears in other surface areas of the field, preserve it as a candidate recurring local pattern rather than assuming it is unique to one formation.

## 4. Decay / extinction ecology lane

The existing crypto terrain work has focused more heavily on emergence, activation, propagation, saturation, rerouting and persistence than on structural death.

A future mirror-image lane should map:

- decline,
- degradation,
- failed recovery,
- rank erosion,
- participation loss,
- slow bleed,
- abrupt collapse,
- zombie/residual survival,
- rehabilitation/reentry,
- and the effect of dying objects on neighboring topology.

The working principle is that an ecosystem map is incomplete without both **birth/emergence** and **death/extinction** dynamics.

## 6. Failed-recovery stress-response concept

A potentially strong future discriminator is not simply how far an asset fell, but how it responds when the relevant environment improves.

Future comparison:

- broad market improves,
- relevant sector/chain improves,
- peer rank band improves,

then ask whether the degraded object:

- participates normally,
- responds weakly,
- responds with delay,
- fails completely,
- recovers rank but not price,
- recovers price but not rank.

A living/healthy object may recover under favorable perturbation while a structurally degraded object may repeatedly fail the same stress-response test.

This may eventually help distinguish:

- accumulation/stall,
- dormant-but-viable,
- decaying/dead.

Do not assume this distinction exists until tested.

## 7. Death can alter the surrounding field

Future work should not stop at:

> Why did this asset/sector die?

Also ask:

> What happens to the surrounding topology when an object loses structural viability?

Possible consequences to map later:

- capital release,
- rank vacancies,
- migration into surviving peers,
- sector concentration,
- volatility spillover,
- liquidity dissipation,
- no observable handoff.

A death event may therefore be both a local state transition and a perturbation to adjacent patches.

## 8. Growth-side and death-side synthesis candidate

Future conceptual lifecycle, still non-canonical:

**EMERGENCE → ACTIVATION → PROPAGATION → SATURATION → DECAY → DEATH / REENTRY / REROUTING**

Local motifs may enter or exit this lifecycle at different points and need not traverse the entire sequence.

The eventual goal is not to force all crypto assets into one universal life cycle, but to map which local patches share which transitions and under what constraints.

## 9. Post-MECH7 deepening lanes — PARKED CONTEXT

Preserve these for the next review cycle after Agent-2 local-event work. They are not active authorization.

### 9.1 Isolated-downside prehistory

Push the isolated-downside clock backward beyond -14D using dense checkpoints such as -30/-21/-14/-10/-7/-5/-3/-2/-1/0.

Primary question:

> What was the field doing while the eventual loner was becoming vulnerable?

Specifically test whether rebound-type shocks show rising dispersion inside otherwise healthy/stable field conditions, while continuation-type shocks show dispersion rising alongside field deterioration. Separate BTC level from BTC stability/recovery/acceleration/deceleration.

### 9.2 Breadth x dispersion full state matrix

Deepen the 2x2 BRD_HI/LO x DISP_HI/LO plane into a full transition/state-age map.

Track all state transitions, dwell, state age, return paths, isolated-vs-coordinated event rates, 1s/2s/3s delivery, reversal, continuation, rank recruitment, concentration, BTC/ETH, volatility, and recurrent re-entry.

Do not force lifecycle labels. Candidate early/mature/exhausted distinctions must be earned from state age and path geometry.

### 9.3 Breadth architecture

Breadth level remains dominant, but decompose who creates it: rank, liquidity, age, volatility, rank-health, tail-active vs normal, and future tradability when the mask exists.

Question:

> Can the same breadth level arise from materially different participation architectures with different later behavior?

Do not undermine breadth level by needless feature proliferation; test composition only for incremental or state-defining information.

### 9.4 Rank-health vs price-recovery clocks

Treat short-horizon price rebound and structural rank health as distinct clocks.

For deteriorating-rank isolated shocks, classify:
- price recovers + rank recovers,
- price recovers + rank keeps deteriorating,
- rank recovers before price,
- neither recovers.

This is a key bridge to future decay/death ecology and temporal delivery. A rebound must not be interpreted as structural health.

### 9.5 SHMC/SHHM narrow follow-up

Do one focused comparison only. SHMC remains dissolved as a high-tail state. Test whether SHMC has a stable reversion-role relative to SHORT_HOT_MEDIUM_HOT continuation-like behavior across breadth, dispersion, rank depth, isolation, and 1s/2s/3s paths. Kill if weak; do not rescue repeatedly.

### 9.6 Volatility provisional role

Park volatility as a possible ignition/intensity/retention coordinate rather than a route/direction selector. Revisit only if it interacts materially with lonely dumps, rank health, 1s timing, or breadth-dispersion states.

### 9.7 HIGH_BREADTH + HIGH_DISPERSION lifecycle

Fully map pre-state -> entry -> dwell/maturation -> exit -> post-state. The state itself is strong; entry/exit choreography is not yet earned.

Track breadth-first vs dispersion-first entry, rank-depth recruitment, tail-share growth, isolated/clustered activity, state age, exit order, reentry, propagation, and post-state behavior. Build a transition lattice rather than force one path.

### 9.8 Lonely-dump neighbor model

A loner is not assumed socially isolated just because same-band same-sign count is one.

Build contextual neighborhoods:
- rank neighbors,
- behavioral neighbors,
- correlation neighbors,
- state neighbors,
- sector/chain only descriptively where appropriate.

Measure the loner's residual against its neighborhood and track what the neighbors do after the shock: stay flat, catch down, catch up, cluster later, contagion, or remain disconnected.

The core future real-time research question is:

> How unusual is this shock relative to the asset's correct local neighborhood at that moment?

Potential stack, still non-executable:

**shock isolation x field health x asset/rank health x temporal recovery/delivery**.

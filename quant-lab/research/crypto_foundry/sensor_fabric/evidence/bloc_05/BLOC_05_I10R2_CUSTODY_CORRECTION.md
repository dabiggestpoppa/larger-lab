### 3.3 What the logical change actually is (normalized, whitespace-insensitive)

Every logical edit is the same authorized extension, applied on the established
I02/I04A/I05/I08/I09 precedent:

- `I08_DISCLOSED_SRC_PREFIX` (a single string) becomes `DISCLOSED_SRC_PREFIXES`
  (a tuple) gaining exactly one entry: `…/normalization/sensors`. The guard
  becomes `any(path.startswith(prefix) for prefix in DISCLOSED_SRC_PREFIXES)` —
  still an allowlist, still exhaustive over changed paths.
- `I10_DISCLOSED_SHA256` gains three entries and `I10_DISCLOSED_EVIDENCE_SHA256`
  gains two; both are merged into the `CURRENT_*` maps by `**` unpacking, so the
  I08/I09 historical pins are **retained, not replaced**.
- `test_b5_i01` adds `"sensors"` to its allowed subpackage set and removes the
  now-authorized directory name from its forbidden-directory list, keeping the
  *module* form forbidden so a flat `sensors.py` still cannot shadow the package.
- `test_b5_i05` / `test_b5_i08` / `test_b5_i09` put `"sensors"` on the
  deferred/authorized side of the same allowlist, with `test_b5_i08` widening
  its source-change assertion to `"/common/" in line or "/sensors/" in line`.

**No assertion was deleted, weakened, skipped or made vacuous.** The negative
path was re-proved at `cd8208ee4a` in I10R1: injecting a fourth module into
`normalization/sensors/` fails `test_sensors_package_is_exactly_the_three_authorized_modules`
precisely, with the offender named in the assertion message.

### 3.4 Disclosure of record

> **The I10E1 commit `384282cbca` converted five sealed historical files from
> LF to CRLF line endings without declaring it.** The conversion is behaviourally
> inert — Python 3 tokenizes both, the suite passes, and `HEAD` bytes equal the
> successor blobs in the table above — but it is a custody event: it changed the
> sha256 of five files that prior checkpoints had sealed, and it multiplied the
> reviewable diff by a factor of roughly 40 (2,667 raw lines for 83 logical
> ones). The raw-line inflation is hereby declared non-authoritative: **only the
> normalized logical change in §3.3 is a change to behaviour, and it is the
> authorized `sensors/` extension.**

### 3.5 Why no normalization was performed here

Restoring LF would change five more sealed hashes, breaking the very pins that
currently prove what the I10 pass did, and would require a simultaneous repin of
the four inventory entries plus `I10_DISCLOSED_SHA256` plus the I06 evidence
pins — a cascade that is *larger* than the defect and would make the historical
chain harder, not easier, to reconstruct. Normalization is a separate,
separately authorized mechanical operation. It is **not** executed here, and no
pin is altered.

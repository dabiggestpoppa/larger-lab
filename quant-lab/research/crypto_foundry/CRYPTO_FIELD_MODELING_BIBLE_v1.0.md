## 1. Purpose

The objective is not merely to preserve research outputs. It is to preserve the **way the research is thought about** so a future agent can reason adjacent to the operator's modeling philosophy instead of only reading finished statistics.

This doctrine is intentionally lighter than a full mathematical treatise. It preserves:

- the order in which questions are asked,
- what kinds of claims are considered strong or weak,
- how observations become structure,
- how local rules coexist with global constraints,
- how timing is separated from direction,
- how direction is allowed to emerge from constraint resolution,
- how nulls and failed ideas are retained,
- how scientific formalisms are selected only after the field earns them,
- how local nodes are accumulated into a larger field map without forcing a grand unified theorem.

A future research agent should be able to read this file and understand not only **what was discovered**, but **how to continue discovering in the same spirit**.

## 2. Core worldview — markets as constraint-resolution fields

Markets are not approached primarily as prediction games.

The research object is a **constraint-resolution field**.

At any moment the field contains:

- available states,
- blocked states,
- active constraints,
- local degrees of freedom,
- state-dependent transition possibilities,
- persistence / decay conditions,
- perturbations,
- release paths,
- reentry paths,
- propagation paths,
- and unresolved hidden state.

The central question is therefore not:

> What will price do next?

but:

> What state exists, what constraints remain active, what transitions are still available, what can survive, what can release, how long can it persist, and where can resolution propagate?

Direction is important, but it is usually a **second-order expression** of a deeper structural resolution.

This principle mirrors CEREBUS-style thinking: structure and constraints dominate; directional sign is often a consequence of the geometry rather than the primary causal object.

---

## 3. Direction doctrine — direction is second order

Do not begin research by asking only UP or DOWN.

Direct directional forecasting is often noisy because it compresses several separate questions into one label.

Prefer to decompose the problem into:

1. **WHAT** state or event exists?
2. **WHEN** does it tend to resolve?
3. **HOW LONG** can it persist?
4. **HOW FAR** does its effect propagate?
5. **WHERE** can it route?
6. **WHAT FAILS** when the expected branch does not occur?
7. **WHICH SIGN** does the realized branch ultimately take?

If the first six are sufficiently constrained, direction often becomes easier to infer using simple sign, correlation, relative-strength, rank, or local-response primitives.

Parking-lot analogy:

- people gathering in the parking lot = capacity / potential,
- moving toward the entrance = activation / recruitment,
- club becoming active = realization,
- full sustained participation = propagation,
- people leaving = decay / release,
- next destination = reentry, rerouting, another local patch, or exit.

The research goal is to know the **available destinations and clocks** before obsessing over which compass direction the car turns.

## 4. Governing discovery grammar

The canonical research grammar is:

**OBSERVE → COMPRESS → PERTURB → RECONSTRUCT → LOCALIZE → FORMALIZE**

### OBSERVE
Describe the phenomenon without assuming mechanism.

### COMPRESS
Remove redundant variables, duplicated descriptions, and proxy clutter.

### PERTURB
Remove, replace, condition, shock, or isolate coordinates to learn what changes.

### RECONSTRUCT
Ask what latent state or mechanism most parsimoniously explains the surviving observations.

### LOCALIZE
Find where the relationship is valid, invalid, stronger, weaker, delayed, or transformed.

### FORMALIZE
Only after the empirical object is stable, choose the mathematics that best describes it.

Never reverse this order merely because an advanced formalism is attractive.

---

## 5. Operator pattern grammar

The preferred compression ladder is:

**INSTANCE → SEQUENCE → PATTERN → FORMATION → STRUCTURE**

### Instance
A single event or local observation.

### Sequence
Ordered instances across time or state transitions.

### Pattern
A recurring sequence or recurring conditional relationship.

### Formation
A more stable configuration composed of multiple patterns or local states.

### Structure
A durable higher-order organization that constrains future possible formations and transitions.

Research should climb this ladder only when evidence supports the next level.

Do not call one instance a pattern.
Do not call one pattern a primitive.
Do not call one formation a universal law.

## 6. Local rules vs global constraints

The field may not have one master primitive.

The preferred architecture is:

**LOOSE GLOBAL CONSTRAINT SYSTEM + LOCAL RULES / PATCHES / PRIMITIVES + CONDITIONAL DEPENDENCIES**

Local rules are not failures of generalization.

A local rule can be scientifically valuable if it is:

- stable in a defined region,
- reproducible,
- non-redundant,
- temporally coherent,
- and explicit about where it fails.

The field may ultimately resemble a road system:

- local streets,
- intersections,
- highways,
- dead ends,
- ramps,
- loops,
- bridges,
- and disconnected regions.

Do not force every road to become one universal bridge.

The long-run goal is to understand enough local roads that the larger transportation network becomes obvious.

The current working hierarchy is:

**GLOBAL CONSTRAINT FIELD**  
→ **BREADTH / DISPERSION STATE**  
→ **STATE AGE / SURVIVAL QUALITY**

A future agent may change the hierarchy only if the empirical field demands it.

Do not preserve hierarchy merely for aesthetic consistency.

## 8. State is not enough — track state age and clocks

A state label alone may be incomplete.

MECH-9/10 showed that dwell time / state age can materially change the meaning of the same state.

Therefore research should distinguish:

- current state,
- state age,
- birth quality,
- survival selection,
- persistence memory,
- exit hazard,
- propagation hazard,
- failure hazard,
- reentry hazard.

Preferred clock lattice:

**ENTRY CLOCK → FAILURE CLOCK → PERSISTENCE CLOCK → PROPAGATION CLOCK → EXIT CLOCK → REENTRY CLOCK**

Do not compress all timing into one average lag.

Time may carry information without being the causal driver of the geometry.

---

## 9. Semi-Markov preference when dwell time matters

When transition probabilities depend on how long a state has persisted, plain first-order Markov logic may be insufficient.

Preferred question:

> Does P(next state | current state, dwell time) materially improve description over P(next state | current state)?

If yes, a semi-Markov / duration-dependent transition lens is natural.

This formalism is preferred because it respects:

- state persistence,
- age-dependent hazards,
- different exit destinations by age,
- rehabilitation / decay clocks,
- route-dependent survival.

Use only if the sample size supports it.

---

## 10. Competing-risk doctrine

Many field outcomes compete.

Do not force them into one success/failure label.

Examples:

- propagation,
- reentry,
- state exit,
- tail activation,
- rehabilitation,
- decay,
- peer contagion,
- local normalization.

Preferred treatment:

**separate outcome clocks + cumulative incidence + conditional hazards**.

The goal is to learn how probability mass migrates as a state survives.

---

## 11. Perturbation doctrine — perturb to learn governing coordinates

Perturbation is not only a robustness check.

It is a discovery method.

For every important node ask:

- What happens if one major variable is removed?
- What happens if a proxy is substituted?
- What happens if the field is conditioned on another state?
- What happens under a small / medium / large perturbation?
- What happens if one cycle, rank band, chain, sector, or peer family is removed?
- Does the effect stay local or propagate?
- Does the response scale smoothly or saturate?
- Does the same perturbation mean something different in young vs mature states?

Perturbation outputs should ideally include:

- response amplitude,
- response latency,
- recovery latency,
- state displacement,
- propagation radius,
- failure mode.

---

## 12. Propagation radius doctrine

An effect can be:

- asset-local,
- peer-local,
- rank-patch-local,
- multi-patch,
- chain-local,
- sector-local,
- field-wide.

Do not assume every valid node propagates broadly.

For important events ask:

> How far does the perturbation travel, how quickly, and through which coordinates?

Useful descriptive outputs include:

- number of peers affected,
- number of rank bands affected,
- maximum rank depth reached,
- breadth change,
- dispersion change,
- tail-share change,
- duration of the propagated effect.

---

## 13. Failure anatomy doctrine

Every important successful path should have a mirror failure study.

Success alone often tells less than divergence between success and failure.

For each promoted path ask:

- what is common to both success and failure,
- what first diverges,
- when divergence begins,
- whether failure is immediate, delayed, or slow,
- whether failure remains local,
- whether failure reroutes,
- whether failure preserves price but loses rank,
- whether favorable field conditions fail to rescue the object.

Common variables across both classes are often **permission variables**.
First diverging variables are more likely to contain mechanism clues.

---

## 14. Health doctrine — price recovery is not structural health

Absolute price and relative structural health are separate clocks.

A price rebound does not imply rank, peer, or structural rehabilitation.

Canonical health-state matrix:

- PRICE_UP + RANK_UP
- PRICE_UP + RANK_DOWN
- PRICE_DOWN + RANK_UP
- PRICE_DOWN + RANK_DOWN

These states should be studied as temporal objects, not static labels.

Questions include:

- does price lead rank,
- does rank lead price,
- does the asset recover only because the broader field is strong,
- does a favorable perturbation rescue it,
- does it stall while peers improve,
- does it relapse,
- does it eventually rehabilitate?

This doctrine is foundational for future decay / death ecology.

---

## 15. Stress-response doctrine

A powerful way to study health is to observe response under favorable perturbation.

Generic experiment:

**FIELD IMPROVES → OBSERVE ASSET RESPONSE**

Possible branches:

- price responds + rank responds,
- price responds + rank fails,
- price stalls + rank decays,
- delayed price response,
- delayed rank rehabilitation,
- no response.

Do not force a pre-event predictor if the separation only becomes visible after the perturbation begins.

A diagnostic process can be valuable even if it is not predictive before t0.

---

## 16. Recovery ladder doctrine

Do not stop at one recovery threshold.

For normalized shocks, track recovery as a ladder where data supports it:

**0.5σ → 1σ → 2σ → 3σ → partial repair → full repair**

Track at multiple horizons rather than one static date.

For each checkpoint ask:

- probability of next checkpoint,
- full repair,
- new low,
- rank recovery,
- peer normalization,
- relapse,
- contagion vs dislocation.

The first meaningful checkpoint may function as a stabilization marker rather than a directional signal.

---

## 17. Peer doctrine — prove the asset is actually alone

Rank-neighbor proximity is not equivalent to a true peer network.

Potential peer systems include:

- rank peers,
- behavioral peers,
- correlation peers,
- state peers,
- hybrid local baskets.

A supposed isolated event should be tested against historically relevant peers before being called a true loner.

Canonical question:

> Did the asset move away from healthy peers, or is the peer group itself entering stress?

Post-event possibilities include:

- ASSET_REJOINS_PEERS,
- PEERS_CATCH_DOWN,
- BOTH_NORMALIZE,
- LOCAL_CONTAGION,
- PERSISTENT_DECOUPLING.

This distinction separates idiosyncratic dislocation from early local contagion.

A basket is a local field instrument.

It may be organized by:

- rank,
- sector,
- chain,
- age,
- liquidity,
- volatility,
- behavior,
- correlation,
- narrative,
- peer state.

A basket is not automatically a trading basket.

Study basket properties such as:

- breadth,
- dispersion,
- median return,
- tail share,
- internal correlation,
- leader concentration,
- rank velocity,
- persistence,
- reversal,
- delivery clocks,
- formation and dissolution.

Possible lifecycle:

**FORMATION → COHERENCE → PARTICIPATION EXPANSION → FRAGMENTATION → DECAY → DISSOLUTION / REFORMATION**

## 19. Triangle / higher-order doctrine

Pairwise relationships may not explain higher-order dependency.

A triangle studies three objects together, for example:

**GLOBAL FIELD × UPPER-FIELD BREADTH × LOWER-FIELD TAIL ACTIVITY**

Before promoting a triangle ask:

- are pairwise relationships already sufficient,
- does the third coordinate add conditional information,
- is the relationship additive, suppressive, synergistic, or redundant,
- does it survive subperiod and definition changes?

Do not expand into hypergraphs, topology, or abstract category structures before simple higher-order relationships are earned.

---

## 20. Redundancy doctrine — compression is a scientific result

The system should become simpler as understanding improves.

When multiple variables encode the same latent information, merge them.

For every feature ask:

- does it add information after the stronger primitive is known,
- does it matter only locally,
- is it just another sensor for the same pressure,
- does removing it change the field map?

Valid outcomes include:

- DISTINCT_INFORMATION,
- CANDIDATE_PRIMITIVE,
- LOCAL_COORDINATE,
- PARTIAL_PROXY,
- REDUNDANT_PROXY,
- NULL,
- PARK.

A variable disappearing after proper control is progress.

## 22. Causality ladder

Never collapse correlation, temporal precedence, mechanism, and causality into one claim.

Use a causal ladder such as:

- L0: descriptive co-occurrence
- L1: conditional association
- L2: temporal lead-lag / predictive ordering under controls
- L3+: increasingly stronger mechanism / intervention evidence where possible

Before causal language ask:

- does A precede B,
- does the order survive state conditioning,
- does it survive common-factor removal,
- does it survive cycle removal,
- is there a plausible transmission path,
- is A necessary, sufficient, substitutable, or merely conditional?

Null causal evidence does not invalidate descriptive geometry.

## 23. Scientific lens selection

Mathematics is selected by the empirical object.

### Survival / competing risks
Use when multiple outcome clocks compete.

### Semi-Markov / renewal
Use when dwell time changes transition probability.

### Dynamical systems
Use for recurrent states, attractor-like regions, corridors, hysteresis, metastability, or stable transition geometry.

### Transfer operators / metastability
Use when the useful object is probability flow between state regions rather than deterministic trajectories.

### Symbolic dynamics
Use when ordered state words / sequences carry structure.

### Information theory
Use for redundancy, incremental information, conditional dependence, entropy, hidden-state reconstruction.

### Graph theory
Use when explicit peer / patch connectivity matters.

### Ecology / metapopulation-style models
Use for health, decay, local extinction, recovery, reentry, and patch interactions if the analogy survives empirically.

### Category theory / sheaf-style architecture
Use mainly as conceptual architecture until empirically supported transformations compose across scales. Do not use as mathematical decoration.

### Topological data analysis
Use only when persistence / connectivity structure cannot be adequately represented with simpler state, graph, or sequence tools.

Advanced mathematics must clarify the field, not impress the reader.

## 24. Data and epistemic discipline

Strict requirements:

- point-in-time truth,
- no future leakage,
- continuous-history features before rank-band filtering where required,
- explicit missingness,
- correct rank sign conventions,
- no survivor backprojection,
- causal rolling windows,
- no fabricated unavailable sensors,
- no replacing DATA_BLOCKED with a weaker proxy and pretending the question was answered.

Distinguish:

**OBSERVED** — directly measured  
**INFERRED** — reconstructed from observations  
**CAUSAL** — supported by stronger evidence  
**UNKNOWN** — unresolved  
**DATA_BLOCKED** — currently unobservable with the available substrate

Never erase uncertainty to make the model look complete.

## 25. Weak/null pattern doctrine

A weak or null relationship may receive **one reasonable conditional recheck** under already-earned regimes.

Examples:

- BTC up/down,
- vol high/low,
- breadth expanding/contracting,
- concentration rising/falling,
- ETH strong/weak,
- risk-on/off,
- HH/HL/LH/LL,
- state age,
- rank depth.

If the relationship remains null, kill or park it.

If it survives only locally, classify it as a local node.

Do not repeatedly rescue favored ideas.

## 26. Locality is a success condition

The discovery of locality is valuable.

A local mechanism can later be tested for:

- perturbation strength,
- propagation radius,
- neighboring-state effects,
- transition paths,
- basket membership,
- temporal delivery.

The long-run field may be reconstructed from many local laws rather than one universal equation.

This is expected, not disappointing.

## 27. Strategy boundary

Terrain and mechanism research come before strategy.

Do not convert every node into a trade rule.

A research object may be useful solely because it identifies:

- state,
- timing,
- constraint,
- local health,
- propagation potential,
- failure risk,
- information limits,
- patch membership,
- or route availability.

Only later, under explicit human authorization, should the same node be split into:

- ALL_EVENTS,
- TRADEABLE_EVENTS,
- LIQUID_TRADEABLE_EVENTS.

Statistical existence is not executable alpha.

## 30. Research-agent question template

Before launching a new checkpoint, the agent should be able to answer:

**OBJECT** — What exactly are we studying?  
**OBSERVATION** — What do we directly observe?  
**UNKNOWN** — What remains hidden or blocked?  
**STATE** — In what field state does the object live?  
**AGE/CLOCK** — What timing / dwell coordinate matters?  
**LOCALITY** — Where is the relationship valid?  
**PERTURBATION** — What happens when the field or object is stressed?  
**FAILURE** — What is the mirror failure path?  
**PEERS/PATCH** — What local neighborhood matters?  
**PROPAGATION** — How far does the effect travel?  
**REDUNDANCY** — What can be merged or removed?  
**FORMALISM** — What is the simplest science capable of expressing the surviving structure?  
**ADJUDICATION** — Promote, merge, dissolve, localize, park, null, or block?

If several of these cannot yet be answered, the next checkpoint should usually improve observation rather than increase model complexity.

## 31. Core philosophical statements

### Constraint before prediction
Understand what the system can and cannot do before predicting which allowed branch will occur.

### Distribution before direction
Map amplitude, timing, tails, persistence, recovery, and coupling before reducing the problem to sign.

### Time carries information
Elapsed time can reveal survival, decay, or transition information without being the underlying causal driver.

### Failure is data
A failed pattern is not discarded until its failure anatomy is understood.

### Local laws matter
A mechanism can be useful even if it applies only to one patch of the field.

### Compression is progress
Fewer non-redundant primitives are preferred to more indicators.

### Nulls protect the model
A preserved null prevents future agents from repeatedly rediscovering the same dead idea.

### Formalism follows evidence
Do not start with topology, category theory, machine learning, or any other tool merely because it is available.

### Direction emerges
When what, when, how long, how far, and where are constrained, directional expression becomes a smaller problem.

### No grand unified theorem required
The field may be irreducibly multi-scale and locally governed.

## 32. Governing summary

The research program seeks a compact, falsifiable, multi-scale ontology of a market field.

Its core method is:

**observe instances → discover sequences → validate patterns → assemble formations → infer structure → perturb structure → localize rules → map clocks → measure propagation → preserve failures → compress redundancy → formalize only what survives.**

The desired agent is not one that memorizes finished conclusions.

The desired agent is one that understands how to ask the next question in a way consistent with this logic.

> Complexity belongs in understanding the field. Direction and execution should inherit only the complexity that the field proves necessary.

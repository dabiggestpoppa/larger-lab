## 1. Core idea

The crypto field should not only be observed through:

- single assets,
- global breadth,
- isolated states,
- or simple pairwise relationships.

A potentially useful next resolution layer is to treat **groups of related objects as local field instruments**.

Working shorthand:

> **Scope the basket. Scope the triangle.**

A basket is not automatically a tradeable basket. It can be a research cohort / local patch used to summarize the state of a portion of the crypto field.

A triangle is not automatically a three-leg trade. It is a higher-order dependency test among three scoped objects.

Do not force compression or invent composite factors prematurely. Let repeated evidence justify every basket and relationship.

---

## 2. Basket as a local field patch

A basket/cohort can be defined by relatively stable structure or dynamic state.

Possible basket families:

### Structural baskets
- rank band
- sector
- chain/ecosystem
- age cohort
- liquidity tier
- market-cap tier
- tradability/perp-listed tier later

### State baskets
- SHORT_HOT_MEDIUM_COLD
- breadth-expanding members
- rank improvers
- rank deteriorators
- high-volatility members
- downside-extreme members
- recovery cohort
- high-tail-potential members

### Functional baskets
- defensive
- tail-active
- propagation leaders
- laggards
- reversal-prone
- high-common-factor
- high-idiosyncratic
- decay / failed-recovery cohorts later

One asset may belong to multiple baskets simultaneously.

Membership may be static or dynamic. Crypto changes too rapidly to assume fixed membership is always appropriate.

For each earned basket, future terrain work may measure:

- breadth
- breadth velocity / acceleration
- median return
- normalized displacement
- dispersion
- skew / tail balance where valid
- tail-event share
- leader concentration
- rank velocity
- internal correlation
- external correlation
- common-factor exposure
- idiosyncratic variance share
- volatility
- participation depth

- reversal geometry
- time-to-delivery
- decay / recovery response
- local-cluster share

The basket therefore acts as a **sensor**, not necessarily as a tradeable portfolio.

---

## 4. Scope-the-basket discipline

Before studying a basket, explicitly define:

- membership rule
- PIT membership method
- static vs dynamic membership
- minimum effective size
- time horizon
- state definition
- internal statistics
- external reference
- persistence / dwell rule
- sample-independence treatment
- lifecycle handling
- later tradability annotation if relevant

Do not form baskets based on future outcome.

Do not use basket construction to hide weak single-asset evidence.

---

## 5. Basket-to-basket relationships

Once baskets are empirically justified, map edges between them.

Examples:

- upper-field breadth basket ↔ lower-field tail basket
- defensive basket ↔ risk-on basket
- rank-improver basket ↔ rank-deteriorator basket
- young-asset basket ↔ mature-asset basket
- sector basket ↔ rank-depth basket

Potential edge types:

- co-movement
- conditioning
- lead-lag
- synchronization
- divergence
- recruitment
- fragmentation
- decay
- rerouting
- common-factor-only
- null

No flow language without flow data.

---

## 6. Scope-the-triangle discipline

For three scoped objects A, B, C, examine:

- A ↔ B
- B ↔ C
- A ↔ C

and especially conditional relationships:

- A ↔ C | B
- B ↔ C | A
- A ↔ B | C

Question whether the third object:

- mediates
- conditions
- amplifies
- suppresses
- inverts
- redundantly restates
- or adds no information.

This is higher-order dependency mapping, not yet mathematical topology and not yet an alpha portfolio.

## 8. Basket lifecycle concept

A basket itself may exhibit a lifecycle:

FORMATION
→ COHERENCE
→ PARTICIPATION EXPANSION
→ SATURATION
→ FRAGMENTATION
→ DECAY
→ DISSOLUTION / REFORMATION

This is provisional.

Potential future use: sector death / recovery, rank-band deterioration, narrative rotation, defensive clustering, and dynamic cohort formation.

Do not force every basket to traverse this lifecycle.

---

## 9. Relationship to later stat-arb research

A stable basket can later provide a reference distribution for individual assets or sub-baskets.

Examples of future research questions:

- asset deviates from basket median
- asset rank deteriorates while basket improves
- basket breadth expands while one member fails to participate
- local tail event occurs without basket confirmation

These may eventually become relative-value / statistical-arbitrage research candidates.

For now, only map normal basket geometry and deviations.

No PnL.
No hedge ratios.
No executable spreads.

## 10. Tradability remains a separate layer

Do not filter terrain discovery by current tradability.

Maintain the distinction:

STATISTICAL_VALIDITY
vs
EXECUTABILITY / TRADABILITY

- option availability

A statistically valid basket or relationship may exist before the market infrastructure becomes tradeable enough to exploit it.
